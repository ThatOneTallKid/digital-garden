Every day, your brain makes thousands of decisions. To make those decisions quickly, it relies on mental shortcuts known as **cognitive biases**. These shortcuts are useful—they save time and mental effort—but they can also distort reality and lead to poor decisions. Here are five of the most common biases worth recognizing.

### **Confirmation Bias**

We naturally look for information that supports what we already believe while ignoring evidence that contradicts it. Instead of asking _“How can I prove I’m right?”_, ask _“What would prove me wrong?”_

### **The Sunk Cost Fallacy**

We continue investing time, money, or effort into something simply because we’ve already invested so much. Remember: past costs are gone. Make decisions based on future value, not past investment.

### **The Dunning–Kruger Effect**

Beginners often overestimate their abilities because they don’t yet understand the complexity of a subject. Ironically, the more you learn, the more you realize how much there is still to know.

### **The Availability Heuristic**

We tend to believe that events we can easily remember are more common than they actually are. Dramatic stories often outweigh statistics in our minds, leading to distorted judgments about risk and probability.

### **The Spotlight Effect**

Most of us think people notice our mistakes far more than they actually do. In reality, everyone is busy thinking about themselves. The typo, awkward moment, or embarrassing slip you keep replaying is probably already forgotten by everyone else.

## **Final Thought**

You can’t eliminate cognitive biases—they’re part of being human. But you can learn to recognize them. The simple habit of pausing to question your first instinct can lead to clearer thinking, better decisions, and a more objective view of the world.
